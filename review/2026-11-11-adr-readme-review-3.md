The rewrite is consistent with itself, with the record and with the rules of today, with one exception that breaks the intro's own promise: the file-name example names 0002. The rest is ten Low points, mostly in sentences written tonight: a rule filed as an example, an arithmetic claim that does not hold in general, three reasons that are cryptic or unfounded, a table cell that carries rules while the intro says tables hold definitions, one Appendix subsection missing from the skeleton, and a literal in the checker.

## Medium

1. **The file-name example depends on a record** (L57–59): "Example: `0002-api-v4-support-jdss1.md`, whose decision is the bold paragraph of its Decision section", while the intro promises examples "that depend on no record" (L21–22). The claim about 0002 is true, but the sentence will need an edit if that record is ever renamed or superseded, which is what the promise exists to prevent. Either the example takes the pattern's shape, "a record about supporting a new API version on one product line is named after that problem, not after the decision", or the intro allows the one exception and says so.

## Low

2. **A rule filed as an example** (L264–266): under "Numbers stay where they mean order or count", the Example reads "another document's numbering, the hurl suite's Findings, a design's items, the testing repository's rows, is cited as that document's". That is a rule of its own, not an illustration of numbers staying. It belongs in the bold sentence or in a bullet of its own, with the hurl suite and the testing repository as its Example.
3. **The rendering arithmetic does not hold in general** (L272–274): "`30.` as an ordered-list item, which renders as 24 when seven numbers before it are gone". A renderer numbers an item by its position, counted from the first item's number; 30 rendered as 24 because it was the third item of a list that started at 22, and between 22 and 30 six numbers were gone, not seven. Say "which renders with the number of its position: 24 for the third item of a list that starts at 22".
4. **Three reasons to reword or drop**, all written tonight: "Why: a review reads the text, not the diff" (L298) is cryptic for "every change keeps the checks green"; "a reviewer reads the whole text, so a change must not leave it broken anywhere" says it. "Often weeks later" (L76) is an empirical claim with nothing behind it; the Why stands without it. "Why: a number cited anywhere keeps its meaning" (L61–62) is the reason for never renumbering, repeated from L263–264; for never reusing a file number the reason is that a superseded record keeps its number and its name next to the record that replaced it.
5. **"Tables hold definitions, not rules"** (L23), while three cells of the Sections table carry rules: "alternatives nobody weighed are not written up to fill the list" and "An option carries what distinguishes it, not the mechanism" (L95), "The mechanism lives here and nowhere else" (L97). They are right where they are, so the intro should say that a rule stated inside a definition is part of it and has no Why of its own.
6. **The skeleton's Appendix lacks the Compliance matrix** (L504–533 of the skeleton): the Appendix row names "the compliance rules and matrix" (L105), the record has `### Compliance matrix` as its own subsection after What "compliant" means, and the skeleton now shows the other fixed subsections. One block and one Table of Contents line.
7. **Two phrasings for one thing**: the What changes row says an item states "what must hold once it is done" (L97) and the Testing section says "the item says only what its tests must prove" (L116); the record's items say "What must hold:". Align the Testing sentence to "what must hold".
8. **The checker's wording list is literal** (`tools/check_record.py`, L137: "design 0002", "ADR 0002") while the README's check says "design NNNN" (L322). For a later record the script would miss "design 0003". A pattern, `design \d{4}` and `ADR \d{4}`.
9. **The registry's area rule has one sentence the README does not**: `kub-testing/issues/README.md` says "A new concern adds a new area word; nothing existing changes". The two lists must stay equal, so the README's area rule should either say the same or send the reader to the registry for how an area is added.
10. **The pointer bullet has no Why** (L83–84) and is not a rule; fine as a cross-reference, but the intro says "every rule below has the same shape". One word: "the rules below".
11. **One overlong line** (L311, the checks sentence) from tonight's edit. Cosmetic.
