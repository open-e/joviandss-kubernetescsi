# ADR 0002: review of the Options considered section

Date: 2026-11-11, night. Reviewed the same way as the Testing section: the section (`docs/adr/0002-api-v4-support-jdss1.md`, lines 346 to 486) read against the README's Options row (two or three real contenders per choice, trade-offs against the priorities by number, the adopted one first and marked, the one-option form with its why and its price, no mechanism, a Decision in bold with a rationale naming each priority and the sentence for a reader with another order of priorities), against the decisions of the last two days (one option for the version, generated types, question 29, the one-series delivery), and against the sections it cites. Report only; nothing in the record edited.

## Verdict

The section does what the README asks. Option A is the one-option form with its why and its price; B1 is first and marked; every option argues against the priorities by number; the rationale names all seven priorities; the sentence for a reader who weighs tooling above drift is there; the Advice keeps the history the section no longer tells. What is left: one question only the user can answer (whether B3, B4 and B5 were real contenders), one place where today's decision of question 29 has not arrived, and three wording points.

## For the user

1. **Five options in group B, where the README asks for two or three real contenders.** B1 and B2 are argued in full; B3 (every field by hand), B4 (generate the whole client) and B5 (tolerate type errors) are two or three lines each, each with one advantage and the priority it fails. The README's rule is the one you pointed at: alternatives nobody weighed are not written up to fill the list. If B3 to B5 were weighed, the section stands and the README's "two or three" is a guideline, not a limit; if any of them was listed to complete the design space, it goes, and its one sentence of why not can stay under B1 or B2. The Advice does not say which; only you can.

## Low

2. **Option A's price predates question 29** (L371–374): "a JovianDSS 1 release without v4, or with a different v4, stops working after the upgrade, with no fallback". Since this afternoon the record says older releases do not serve the same v4 and are not supported. The price is now definite: every older JovianDSS 1 release stops working after the upgrade, with no backward compatibility ([question 29], [risk 2]). The bold Decision (L449–459) says "the minimum supported release" and could say in four words that nothing older is supported; question 29 is the one question decided on group A's own subject, and the Decision's list of question decisions (L473–483) does not name it.
3. **"mapped from its recorded message only"** (L474–476) against the catalogue it links: `impl_plugin_rest_error_catalogue` maps from status, `class`, `errno` and message pattern, and keeps two legacy patterns no recording supports, marked "no recording". One phrase: "from its recorded status, class, errno and message, with the two unrecorded legacy patterns kept and marked".
4. **The intro's account of the other decisions** (L349–353): "taken as questions, each with its alternatives in its resolution; the Decision lists them and the Decisions table keeps them". The table holds one line per decision and no alternatives, by the README's own rule, and the Decision names six of the twenty-seven. Say "each decided as a question; the Decision names the ones that shape the delivery, and the Decisions table keeps them all".
5. **B1 restates three rules of Table 2** (L379–385: exact read fields, a missing required one fails by name, everything else raw). The README says an option carries what distinguishes it, not the mechanism. These three are what distinguishes it, so this is a trim at most: the first sentence and the link to the item would carry the same.
6. **B4 counts "the 36 call functions"** (L431): the carried-over item 20; whatever definition is chosen applies here too.

## Verified and found consistent

- Form: the intro names the two groups and says which has one option and which has contenders; Option A says it is the only one, why no other was weighed (the Problem statement's deprecation, key observations 1 and 2) and what it costs; B1 is first and marked "(adopted)"; B2 to B5 state advantages and disadvantages against priorities 2, 3 and 5 by number; the Decision is bold, the rationale names priorities 1 to 7, and the sentence for another order of priorities names B2 with its price under it; no dates in the section (they are in Advice); the four option anchors and the Decision anchor are in the Table of Contents and resolve.
- Content against the rest of the record: "v3 is deprecated" is the Problem statement's first sentence; "the move changes no behaviour" is key observation 2 and the Measurements table; "19 type names", "the twelve caller lines", "the accessors stay hand-written" are Table 2 and `impl_plugin_rest_generated_reply_types`; "two commits that can each be reverted alone" is priority 5 and stages 1 and 2; "four parameters" are the Advice's user decision of 2026-11-10; B2's "questions 27 and 28" are rows 27 and 28 of the Decisions table; B1's disadvantages (tooling, the user's Node tools, the moving output format, per-endpoint types) are risks 19, 20 and 21 and question 31; the second Decision paragraph's six question decisions match rows 7, 19, 20, 21, 23, 24 and 26.
- History: the Advice records that the first version listed "keep v3" and "support both with a setting", that the user ruled them out, that the rule per field came first and generation was set aside until the spec format settled; the memory of the user's decision says the same.

## Outcome

2026-11-12: the user ruled that Options considered lists strategies, and that B1 to B5 were nuances of one implementation, not strategies. The five subsections were removed; Option A stays as the one strategic matter; the types choice is question 28 of the Decisions table, whose resolution now carries the four alternatives with why each lost and the price of the chosen one, and whose "where it is argued" is the generated-types implementation item; the six references to Option B1 or B2 elsewhere point at question 28 or the item; the Decision lost its "a reader who weighs the absence of tooling above drift would choose Option B2" sentence. Item 1 of this review (B3 to B5 not real contenders) is closed by that; of item 6 (the 36 calls) nothing changed; items 2 to 5 are open except that B1's restatement of the Table 2 rules went with B1 and the introduction no longer claims that the Decision lists every question.
